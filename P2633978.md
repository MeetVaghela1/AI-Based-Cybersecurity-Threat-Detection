### Introduction 

**Statement of Problem** With sophisticated cyberattacks on the rise globally, and cybercrime damages expected to cost more than $10.5 trillion annually by 2025, traditional signature-based detection methods are proving ineffective against zero-day exploits, APTs and polymorphic malware. 

**Background and Rationale** We can leverage machine learning to detect evolving threats adaptively. This project explores the performance of four ML algorithms (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost) on NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017 datasets to address gaps in cross-dataset performance, computational efficiency, and deployment guidelines.. 

#### **Gaps in Current Knowledge & Core Issues** 

- Most studies evaluate on a single dataset, limiting cross-dataset robustness conclusions. 

- The real-time trade-off between model complexity and detection latency is not well studied. 

- There is still little practical guidance on how to integrate ML into the live security infrastructure of organisations. 

#### **Possible Applications** 

- Organizational cybersecurity infrastructure: real-time network intrusion detection 

- Automated threat classification and alerts for security operations centers (SOCs) 

- An evidence-based deployment framework for the IT security teams and academic cyber security research community 

### Research Question, Aim & Objectives 

**Research Question** How effective are Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost in detecting cybersecurity threats based on network traffic analysis, and what factors influence detection performance in different dataset settings? 

**Research Aim** The aim of this study was to develop, evaluate and compare ML-based models for detecting cybersecurity threats using network traffic analysis, providing a comprehensive and deployable framework for organisational threat detection and prevention.. 

###### **Research Objectives** 

- Download and prepare datasets NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017 to generate high quality training/testing data (Weeks 1-4) 

- Use correlation analysis, mutual information, and recursive feature elimination to engineer optimal feature subsets 

- Systematic hyperparameter tuning of 4 ML classifiers using grid search and k-fold cross validation 

- • Evaluate and compare models with accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and AUC-ROC to find the best performing algorithm 

- Development of practical deployment guidelines for implementation of the framework into organisational cybersecurity infrastructure. 

**Deliverable** Proposal report, fully functional ML models (Python/GitHub), performance comparison report and deployment guidelines 

**Beneficiaries** Organizational security teams, IT administrators and the cybersecurity research community who are looking for evidence-based and cost-effective threat detection solutions 

## Findings of the Literature Review 

##### • **Theme 1: Deep Learning vs Classical ML** 

- **Mahdavifar & Ghorbani (2019) / Berman et al. (2019):** Deep learning outperforms classical ML on benchmarks but has high computational complexity. Lighter classical algorithms are more practical for real-time deployment. 

##### • **Theme 2: Ensemble Methods Performance** 

- **Almogren & Hassan (2021) / Ramu (2025):** Random forest (94.3%) and XGBoost (95.1 97.2%) provide competitive accuracy at a significantly lower computational cost. Sequential error correction in XGBoost deals well with class imbalance. 

##### • **Theme 3: Feature Engineering & Dataset Quality** 

- **Thakkar & Lohiya (2021):** Optimal feature subsets decrease computational complexity by 40% without accuracy loss; dataset quality significantly affects generalisability across heterogeneous network environments 

##### • **Research Gaps This Project Addresses** 

- Evaluation on two datasets ( NSL-KDD + CICIDS2017 ) to analyze robustness across datasets . Direct measurement of computational efficiency . Structured recommendations for deployment 

## Research Design 

**Research Philosophy** Positivist – systematic observation and statistical evaluation of objective, reproducible ML benchmarks. Interpretivist rejected. (no subjective human experience). 

**Research Approach** Deductive –theory in ML and cybersecurity established forms testable hypothesis validated through controlled experimentation. Inductive rejected as less rigorous because of well established foundations of the algorithm.. 

**Research Strategy** The study employed an experimental design with a comparative analysis, training four classifiers under controlled conditions and using the same dataset splits and evaluation protocols. 

**Research Methods** upervised classification on labelled benchmark datasets (NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017) Unsupervised and semi-supervised alternatives were considered and rejected for their reduced interpretability and implementation complexity. 

**Time Horizon** Cross-sectional – data collected and models evaluated in a fixed 16-week project time frame. 

###### **Tools & Techniques** 

- Preprocessing: median imputation, min-max scaling, one-hot encoding, SMOTE for class imbalance 

- Algorithms: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost (Python / scikit-learn) 

- Evaluation: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, AUC-ROC; grid search + k-fold CV; Google Colab for compute 

## Risks, Ethics and Issues 

###### • **Risks** 

- Dataset Unavailable/corrupted (Low likelihood, High impact) – Mitigation: Get early; backup datasets UNSW-NB15 and KDD Cup 99 

- Model overfitting (Medium, High) – Mitigation:  k-fold CV, regularisation, ensemble diversity 

- Computational limitations (Medium, Medium) – Mitigation: Google Colab, code optimisation, incremental training 

- Schedule slippage (Medium, Medium) – Mitigation: phased timeline with buffer weeks in Phases 3 and 4 

###### • **Ethical Issues** 

- Data privacy: uses only publicly available, anonymised benchmark datasets (no PII); fully GDPR compliant - no ethics board approval necessary 

- Dual-use risk: mitigated by only focusing on detection, no documentation of offensive capability, dissemination through academic channels with defensive framing (BCS/ACM standards) 

###### • 

###### **Other Issues** 

- Evaluation bias risk: mitigated by uniform evaluation protocol for all models and multiple performance metrics
